## What was done
- Updated the content consolidation logic within the `GeminiChat` class for handling streaming responses.
- Changed the text appending mechanism from targeting the first part to the last text part of existing multi-part content.
- Introduced two new helper methods, `isFirstPartText` and `isLastPartText`, to precisely identify whether the first or last part of a `Content` object is pure text.
- Enhanced `isLastPartText` to validate that the content role is 'model' and to explicitly exclude non-text part types like `functionCall`, `functionResponse`, `inlineData`, `fileData`, and `thought`.
- Refactored `isFirstPartText` to also ensure the content role is 'model' and to exclude non-text part types, improving type safety and accuracy.

## Impact
- Users will experience more accurate and correctly consolidated text in streaming multi-part responses from the Gemini model, avoiding garbled or incorrectly merged output.
- Improves the reliability and consistency of how text content is processed and displayed, especially in mixed-content streaming scenarios.

## Technical details
- Modified the `handleStreamingChunk` method in `packages/core/src/core/geminiChat.ts` to adjust how `Content` objects are merged and appended.
- The core change involved adapting the logic to append new text to `lastContent.parts[lastContent.parts.length - 1].text` rather than `lastContent.parts[0].text`.
- The new helper methods, `isFirstPartText` and `isLastPartText`, leverage explicit checks for `content.role` and ensure that `text` properties are not present alongside other specific content types (`functionCall`, `inlineData`, etc.) to prevent misidentification of text parts.
