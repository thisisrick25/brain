## What was done
- Refactored the `consolidateStreamingContent` logic in `packages/core/src/core/geminiChat.ts` to correctly append new text parts to the *last* existing text part of multi-part content.
- Introduced two new private helper methods: `isFirstPartText` and `isLastPartText`, to precisely identify whether the first or last part of a `Content` object is a pure text part.
- Enhanced `isLastPartText` to include checks for `model` role and explicitly exclude non-text content types such as `functionCall`, `functionResponse`, `inlineData`, `fileData`, and `thought`.
- Updated `isFirstPartText` with similar explicit checks to ensure the first part is exclusively text and not other content types.
- Replaced the simpler `isTextContent` checks with the new, more precise `isFirstPartText` and `isLastPartText` in the consolidation logic.

## Impact
- Resolves incorrect text concatenation issues in streaming multi-part responses, ensuring text from different content chunks merges seamlessly.
- Improves the reliability and accuracy of handling mixed content types within Gemini chat interactions.
- Enhances the robustness of the content consolidation mechanism for streaming generative AI responses.

## Technical details
- The core change involves modifying how `Content` objects, especially in streaming scenarios, append new `Part` data within the `GeminiChat` class.
- The `consolidateStreamingContent` method now checks the `isLastPartText` of the accumulated content and `isFirstPartText` of the new content to determine if simple text concatenation is appropriate, specifically targeting the last text part.
- The helper methods `isFirstPartText` and `isLastPartText` leverage TypeScript type guards and explicit property checks to differentiate pure text parts from other structured parts (e.g., `functionCall`, `inlineData`). This prevents misidentifying parts that might have a `text` property but are semantically different.
